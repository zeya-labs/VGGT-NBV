\begin{abstract}
Next-best-view (NBV) prediction is typically formulated either as a heuristic search problem over hand-crafted visibility utilities or as a task-specific learning problem tied to a bespoke scene representation. Meanwhile, recent feed-forward geometry foundation models can recover multi-view structure, depth, and point maps directly from sparse posed observations, but they are rarely used as frozen priors inside an end-to-end NBV training loop. We present \method, a reconstruction-aware NBV framework derived from the architecture implemented in the \codebase repository. \method couples a frozen multi-view geometry prior with a lightweight attention policy that consumes cross-view scene tokens and predicts the next camera position from a small set of initial observations. The predicted position is converted into a shell-constrained look-at pose, rendered with a differentiable mesh renderer, and scored through a reconstruction objective defined on point clouds sampled from the ground-truth mesh. The framework is modular: it supports multiple scene encoders, multiple reconstruction backends, dynamic view rendering from House3K meshes, and direct comparison against a random-view baseline under GeomLoss, Chamfer Distance, DCD, and EMD. This paper draft documents the method faithfully to the current codebase and provides a CVPR-style manuscript scaffold for the final empirical study.
\end{abstract}
